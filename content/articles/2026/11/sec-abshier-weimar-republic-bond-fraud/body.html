LOS ANGELES — Sovereign debt issued by the German Weimar Republic, Imperial China, and pre-revolutionary Russia has spent more than a century as framed wall decor and flea-market curiosity. According to the Securities and Exchange Commission, two California promoters nonetheless raised more than $3.85 million from at least 85 investors by pitching a secret, government-backed redemption program for the defunct paper—and have now settled the agency’s civil suit for 9.2 cents on the dollar.

In Litigation Release No. 26688, published Nov. 19, the S.E.C. announced that the U.S. District Court for the Central District of California entered final consent judgments against Billy Abshier and Christopher W. Abshier, operators of Historic Asset Placement Services Global, LLC (HAPS). Between January 2017 and August 2023, the agency’s December 2024 complaint alleged, the defendants told bondholders that placing qualifying historical certificates into HAPS custodial care would unlock an immediate $250,000 advance payment followed by redemption payouts in the millions of dollars, supposedly arranged alongside U.S. and foreign governments and major accounting firms.

Zero Bonds Redeemed, and a Whistleblower-Impediment Injunction

According to the S.E.C., the redemption program was a complete sham: no $250,000 advance was ever issued, and not a single historical bond was ever redeemed through the HAPS process. The complaint further alleged that Christopher W. Abshier and HAPS took steps to impede clients from communicating directly with the S.E.C. about potential federal securities law violations.

Without admitting the allegations in the S.E.C.’s complaint, Billy Abshier and Christopher W. Abshier consented to permanent antifraud injunctions under Section 10(b) and Rule 10b-5, a bar on participating in securities offerings outside their personal accounts, and—for Christopher W. Abshier—an injunction against violating Rule 21F-17’s whistleblower-protection rule. The court ordered civil penalties of $236,451 against Billy Abshier and $118,225 against Christopher W. Abshier, totaling $354,676. The court clerk previously entered a default against HAPS in October 2025 after the entity failed to answer the complaint, while S.E.C. litigation continues against other co-defendants.
