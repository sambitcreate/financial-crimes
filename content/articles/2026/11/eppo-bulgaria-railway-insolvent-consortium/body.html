SOFIA — Winning a €213.7 million European Union railway modernization contract ordinarily requires proving that every member of the bidding consortium is financially solvent. According to an indictment filed at the Sofia City Court on Nov. 19, one participating company cleared that hurdle by declaring it was not in insolvency proceedings—despite having been declared insolvent seven years earlier, in 2012.

The European Public Prosecutor’s Office (E.P.P.O.) in Sofia charged the legal representative of one of the three companies in the winning consortium over a grant agreement worth €213,714,235.14, awarded in 2019 to modernize sections of the railway line linking Sofia and Plovdiv. Co-financed by the European Union’s Connecting Europe Facility and Bulgaria’s national budget, the project resulted in €68,213,186.03 in disbursements from Bulgaria’s National Company Railway Infrastructure (N.R.I.C.) between 2019 and 2024.

An Unwitting Intermediary and a Seven-Year Insolvency

Prosecutors allege that the defendant used the consortium’s manager—who, according to the E.P.P.O., had no knowledge of the alleged scheme—to submit false documentation to N.R.I.C. attesting that his company was not subject to insolvency proceedings. Evidence gathered during the investigation indicates the firm had already been declared insolvent in 2012, rendering the consortium ineligible for the grant under tender rules.

The case originated from a crime report and complementary inquiry by the European Anti-Fraud Office (O.L.A.F.), followed by searches and document seizures across company headquarters and private residences in Bulgaria conducted with the National Police General Directorate and the State Agency for National Security. As the E.P.P.O. emphasized in its Nov. 19 announcement, all persons concerned are presumed innocent until proven otherwise in competent Bulgarian courts.
