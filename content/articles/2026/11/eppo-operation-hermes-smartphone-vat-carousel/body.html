LUXEMBOURG — Under a tax scheme busted by European prosecutors in October 2026, crates of smartphones enjoyed a better European tour than most tourists — looping across eight E.U. countries and collecting value-added tax refunds at every border.

In “Operation Hermes,” the European Public Prosecutor’s Office staged more than 300 dawn raids on Oct. 7, 2026, across Portugal, Spain, Italy, Austria, France, Germany, Malta, and the Netherlands. Investigators detained 62 suspects linked to two alleged syndicates, secured €310 million in freezing orders, and seized €9 million in physical assets against an estimated €410 million ($445 million) in missing V.A.T. revenue.

Spinning the Border Loophole

V.A.T. carousel fraud exploits a dry quirk of the E.U. single market: cross-border wholesale shipments are zero-rated, leaving tax collection to the destination country. According to the E.P.P.O., the syndicates routed smartphones through “missing trader” shell wholesalers that charged domestic V.A.T. and vanished before paying the treasury.

Allied exporters further down the invoice chain then filed legitimate refund claims when shipping the exact same pallets across the next border — turning one batch of phones into a perpetual cash machine without a single handset ever reaching a retail buyer.

All 62 detainees remain in pre-trial proceedings and are presumed innocent until convicted. Even with €310 million in freezing orders, the gap between €9 million actually seized in hand and €410 million missing from public treasuries captures the math of European tax crime: paper crates still outrun tax collectors.
