LUXEMBOURG — In a tax scheme European prosecutors say they disrupted in October 2026, mobile phones allegedly enjoyed a better European tour than most tourists — passing through chains of companies across the E.U. and generating value-added tax refunds along the way.

In “Operation Hermes,” the European Public Prosecutor’s Office staged more than 300 dawn raids on Oct. 7, 2026, across Portugal, Spain, Italy, Austria, France, Germany, Malta, and the Netherlands. Investigators detained 62 suspects in Portugal, Spain and Italy linked to two alleged syndicates, executed orders to seize up to €310 million, and seized assets worth up to €9 million against an estimated €410 million ($445 million) in V.A.T. losses.

Spinning the Border Loophole

V.A.T. carousel fraud exploits a dry quirk of the E.U. single market: cross-border wholesale shipments are zero-rated, leaving tax collection to the destination country. According to the E.P.P.O., the syndicates allegedly traded the goods through a chain of intermediary companies and “missing traders” that vanished without meeting their tax obligations.

Other companies further down the chain then claimed V.A.T. reimbursements from national tax authorities, prosecutors allege — the loop that gives a carousel fraud its name, and that can turn one batch of phones into a cash machine if no one stops the ride.

All 62 detainees remain in pre-trial proceedings and are presumed innocent until convicted. Even with orders to seize up to €310 million, the gap between up to €9 million actually seized in hand and an estimated €410 million missing from public treasuries captures the math of European tax crime: paper crates still outrun tax collectors.
