MIAMI — After pausing corporate foreign-bribery cases, the Justice Department is back in business across Latin American supply chains — mixing half-off corporate settlements with its first criminal charge under the Foreign Extortion Prevention Act in November 2026.

Following the department’s post-Blanche Memo policy shift, prosecutors in the Southern District of Florida and the Fraud Section have traded broad global F.C.P.A. policing for a narrower rule: bring the bribery case when cash bags, border corridors, or cartels show up in the files.

Half Off, No Monitor

The flagship deal landed in November 2025, when TIGO Guatemala signed a $118.2 million deferred prosecution agreement in a case tied to bags of cash and a cartel link. Even with those details on the docket, TIGO walked away with a 50 percent cooperation discount, zero compliance monitor, and no criminal conviction.

Eight months later, in July 2026, The Scoular Company resolved bribery tied to its Mexican border operations with a $10.18 million deferred prosecution agreement. Neither corporate deal charged an executive.

Then, in November 2026, prosecutors opened a demand-side ledger with the inaugural charge under the Foreign Extortion Prevention Act, targeting foreign officials accused of soliciting bribes from U.S.-linked companies. That charge remains a pending allegation, and the accused is presumed innocent. For multinational general counsel, the boundary line is now unmistakable: bribery enforcement has resumed, provided cartels appear somewhere on the manifest.
