DALLAS — Multi-factor authentication is supposed to stop a stranger from draining a bank account with nothing more than a stolen password. According to a 22-count federal indictment unsealed in Dallas on Nov. 20, a 14-defendant syndicate bypassed that safeguard by recruiting corrupt insiders inside major U.S. banks and telecommunications carriers—and compiling a spreadsheet of nearly 3,000 prospective victims whose accounts held more than $370 million.

The U.S. Attorney’s Office for the Northern District of Texas charged Muhamed Dieye, 35, of Dallas—who allegedly operated under aliases including “MJ,” “JayJay,” “Medici,” and “Lambomotion Tony”—alongside 13 co-defendants with conspiracy to commit wire fraud and bank fraud, substantive bank and wire fraud, and aggravated identity theft. Prosecutors allege the ring ran from roughly July 2023 through November 2024.

SIM Swaps, Insiders, and a One-Way Ticket at J.F.K.

Court filings describe an industrial-grade takeover assembly line. Prosecutors allege that members of the conspiracy combined stolen personal and business records, counterfeit identification documents, phone-number spoofing, and SIM swapping with insider assistance inside major financial institutions and telecom providers to intercept security alerts and clear multi-factor verification. Institutions targeted or named in connection with the investigation included JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America, Truist Bank, Charles Schwab, First Bank, and Woodforest National Bank.

According to prosecutors, Dieye maintained a master spreadsheet listing personal and business data for nearly 3,000 individuals whose bank balances collectively topped $370 million ($373 million across targeted accounts). Federal authorities arrested Dieye at John F. Kennedy International Airport in New York as he allegedly scanned his boarding pass for a one-way flight to Malaysia after shipping belongings to Kenya.

As the Department of Justice noted in its Nov. 20 announcement, an indictment is merely an allegation, and all 14 defendants are presumed innocent unless and until proven otherwise in federal court.
