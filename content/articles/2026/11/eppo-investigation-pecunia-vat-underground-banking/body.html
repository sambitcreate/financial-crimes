MUNICH — More than 3,000 police, tax, and customs officers executed over 300 searches across 12 European countries on Nov. 26 in “Investigation Pecunia,” a sweeping European Public Prosecutor’s Office operation targeting an alleged €120 million underground banking and value-added-tax invoice mill.

According to EPPO’s Munich office, 21 suspects were arrested in Germany, Belgium, and Estonia during coordinated raids spanning Belgium, Croatia, Estonia, France, Germany, Italy, Lithuania, Poland, Romania, Slovakia, the Netherlands, and Moldova. Officers seized large amounts of cash, luxury cars, watches, jewellery, paper records, and digital storage devices.

Twenty-Two Shell Companies and Phantom Service Invoices

Prosecutors allege the group controlled at least 22 shell companies registered in Germany whose sole business was issuing invoices for services that were never performed, attaching fictitious VAT charges to non-existent work. Client firms and individuals allegedly purchased those invoices to claim unearned VAT input deductions on their own tax filings.

On the other side of the ledger, the shell companies allegedly failed to declare the incoming revenue or remit the VAT collected—an arrangement EPPO estimates caused at least €18.9 million in German tax losses while laundering at least €120 million overall.

Cash Withdrawals and Off-the-Books Security Payrolls

Once client payments landed in the shell accounts, investigators allege the funds were withdrawn in physical cash and partially cycled back to the companies and individuals who had bought the fictitious invoices. In at least one documented instance, EPPO said the returned cash was allegedly used to pay employees of private security contractors without registering the workers for social security.

The action brought together Europol, Germany’s Federal Police (Bundespolizei), Customs’ undeclared-work division (Finanzkontrolle Schwarzarbeit), state tax investigators across Rhineland-Palatinate, Saxony, and Thuringia, Germany’s Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU), and financial supervisor BaFin. EPPO emphasized that all 21 arrested individuals are presumed innocent until proven otherwise in competent German courts.
