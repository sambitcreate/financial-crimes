NEW YORK — A Manhattan Supreme Court justice has ordered Cynthia and Eddy Petion, the founders of the collapsed cryptocurrency platform NovaTechFx, to pay $1,367,023,585 in disgorgement and prejudgment interest after the couple defaulted in civil court and fled the United States.

Announced Nov. 25 by New York Attorney General Letitia James and entered in Commercial Division Part 45 by Justice Anar Rathod Patel (Index No. 451633/2024), the monetary judgment caps a Martin Act and Executive Law § 63(12) enforcement lawsuit accusing the Petions of running a sprawling cryptocurrency Ponzi and pyramid scheme that swept up hundreds of thousands of investors worldwide—including more than 11,000 New Yorkers.

Prayer Groups, WhatsApp Chats, and a $1.13 Billion Ledger

According to the Attorney General’s June 2024 complaint and the court’s subsequent default findings, the Petions promoted NovaTechFx and an earlier venture, AWS Mining Pty Ltd., directly inside immigrant communities—particularly Haitian New Yorkers—via religious prayer groups, WhatsApp group chats, and social media pitches promising outsized passive trading returns.

When the Petions failed to answer the lawsuit or appear for the monetary inquest after leaving the country, Justice Patel entered a default judgment in August 2025 permanently banning them from New York’s securities, commodities, and cryptocurrency industries and directing a full investor accounting within 30 days. After the defendants ignored that deadline as well, the court quantified the monetary award from state affirmations filed Oct. 21, 2026.

“NovaTech cruelly targeted immigrant families through faith and community groups with the promise of a better life while robbing them of their life savings.” — Letitia James, New York Attorney General

Stacked Disgorgement, Statutory Interest, and an Empty Dock

Under the signed order (NYSCEF Doc. No. 165), Nova Tech Ltd, NovaTech Advisors, LLC, NovaPay, LLC, and Cynthia and Eddy Petion are jointly and severally liable for $1,134,549,691 in disgorgement plus $232,473,894 in statutory 9% prejudgment interest running from June 1, 2023 through Sept. 10, 2025—totaling $1,367,023,585.

Justice Patel also entered two companion AWS Mining awards carrying 9% prejudgment interest dating back to June 2019: $4,694,712 against Cynthia Petion ($3,000,000 disgorgement plus $1,694,712 interest) and $1,455,666 against AWS Mining Pty Ltd ($930,195 disgorgement plus $525,471 interest). Earlier in the litigation, the Attorney General’s office secured nearly $6 million and permanent industry bans against four other scheme promoters—Martin Zizi, Frantz Ciceron, Ricardo Roy, Sr., and James Corbett.

As a civil default judgment, the ten-figure award arrives with no admission of liability, no criminal custody, and a collection challenge common to offshore crypto collapses: the defendants left the jurisdiction long before the clerk stamped the order.
