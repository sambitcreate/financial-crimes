LOS ANGELES — Most embezzlers attempt at least a token nod toward plausible deniability when naming the bank account receiving stolen client funds. Joshua Mandel, a 47-year-old Woodland Hills film production accountant, chose “Fun Fun Fun.”

On Nov. 23, U.S. District Judge John F. Walter sentenced Mandel—the operator of First J Productions Inc.—to 41 months in federal prison and ordered him to pay $1,747,935 in restitution after he pleaded guilty to six counts of wire fraud. According to the U.S. Attorney’s Office for the Central District of California’s Nov. 23 announcement, Mandel siphoned more than $1.7 million between 2019 and 2023 from roughly 17 independent film production companies that had hired him to manage cash flow, payroll, and vendor invoices.

A Studio Shell Game Paid in Prepaid Cards

Federal prosecutors detailed how Mandel exploited his signatory authority over production bank accounts and CASHét prepaid production debit cards to write unauthorized checks, initiate covert wire transfers, and load money into the “Fun Fun Fun” card account under his personal control. When bills came due on one movie whose treasury he had raided, Mandel ran a classic Hollywood kiting loop—draining cash from another independent production to keep vendors quiet.

Rather than funding set construction or crew payroll, prosecutors said, Mandel spent the diverted film budgets on Las Vegas hotels, clubs, and shows, Louis Vuitton merchandise, hundreds of thousands of dollars in payments to young women including adult-film performers, and more than $129,000 sent to a woman he met on a “sugar daddy” website.

Judge Walter’s judgment pairs nearly three and a half years in federal custody with dollar-for-dollar restitution of $1,747,935—a relatively brisk accounting on the financial-crimes ledger, even if independent producers rarely recover full value once the Las Vegas hotel bills have already cleared.
