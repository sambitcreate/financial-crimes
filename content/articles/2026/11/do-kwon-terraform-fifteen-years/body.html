NEW YORK — Do Kwon got 15 years in federal prison on Dec. 11, 2025, for engineering a $40 billion cryptocurrency wipeout. Spread across roughly 5,475 days behind bars, the math comes to $7.3 million in erased investor wealth per day served.

Judge Paul Engelmayer handed down the sentence in the Southern District of New York, four months after the Terraform Labs co-founder pleaded guilty to federal fraud charges stemming from the 2022 collapse of TerraUSD and LUNA. Alongside the criminal term sits a $4.47 billion civil judgment with the Securities and Exchange Commission.

When Losses Outrun a Human Lifespan

Before it imploded, Terraform pitched TerraUSD and LUNA as an algorithmic stablecoin that needed neither bank reserves nor traditional collateral — just confidence and code. When the peg snapped, $40 billion vanished in days, wiping out retail savers and institutional funds across the globe.

Fifteen years in federal custody is real prison time, far harsher than the deferred deals handed to banks that launder billions. Yet mega-cap fraud exposes the comical ceiling of sentencing arithmetic. Steal $1,800 from a store register and draw two years, and you serve prison time at $2.47 a day. Vaporize $40 billion in tokens, and the per-day discount runs into the millions.

Mr. Kwon’s August 2025 guilty plea spared Manhattan a months-long trial and closed the criminal chapter of Terra’s demise. The docket leaves behind a clean equation: $40 billion gone, 180 months ordered, and a stark reminder that if you are going to blow up other people’s money, scale is still the ultimate bulk discount.
