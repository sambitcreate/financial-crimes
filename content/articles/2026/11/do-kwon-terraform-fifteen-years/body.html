NEW YORK — Do Kwon, the co-founder and chief executive of Terraform Labs whose cryptocurrency ecosystem collapsed in a $40 billion wipeout, was sentenced on Dec. 11, 2025, in Manhattan federal court to 15 years in prison — a punishment that works out to roughly $7.3 million in investor losses for every day he is scheduled to spend behind bars.

The sentence, imposed by Judge Engelmayer in the United States District Court for the Southern District of New York, followed Mr. Kwon’s August 2025 guilty plea to federal criminal charges arising from the collapse of Terraform Labs’ TerraUSD and LUNA digital tokens. Even by the standards of Manhattan’s white-collar criminal docket, the scale of the financial destruction outstripped virtually every conventional benchmark used to measure prison terms.

For federal judges tasked with translating multi-billion-dollar market collapses into calendar years, modern cryptocurrency prosecutions have exposed an awkward boundary of sentencing arithmetic. Human lifespans are finite; digital-asset market capitalizations, during speculative booms, briefly appeared not to be.

The Arithmetic of a $40 Billion Collapse

Before its implosion, Terraform Labs promoted TerraUSD and its sister token, LUNA, as the centerpiece of a self-stabilizing digital currency system. When the design failed, approximately $40 billion in market value evaporated, inflicting sweeping losses on retail and institutional cryptocurrency investors worldwide.

Measured strictly in prison years, a 15-year federal sentence represents a substantial deprivation of liberty, placing Mr. Kwon among the more heavily sentenced figures of the post-boom cryptocurrency crackdown. Yet divided across the roughly 5,475 days of a 15-year term, the $40 billion investor wipeout yields an extraordinary ratio: approximately $7.3 million in erased wealth for each calendar day of incarceration.

Following Do Kwon’s August 2025 guilty plea in the Southern District of New York, Judge Engelmayer imposed a 15-year federal prison sentence on Dec. 11, 2025, closing the criminal sentencing chapter of the $40 billion TerraUSD and LUNA collapse.

Sentencing Scale in the White-Collar Docket

The contrast between individual criminal penalties and aggregate investor harm has become a defining feature of the Southern District of New York’s digital-asset prosecutions. While defendants convicted of modest retail thefts or small-scale frauds routinely face months or years in prison for losses measured in the thousands of dollars, the math of mega-cap financial collapses compresses billions of dollars into each year of a federal sentence.

Mr. Kwon’s August 2025 guilty plea spared the court a sprawling trial, and Judge Engelmayer’s December sentencing formally closed the criminal adjudication of Terraform Labs’ demise. What remains on the public ledger is an equation that speaks without commentary: $40 billion gone, 15 years ordered, and a daily rate of $7.3 million in vanished investor capital.
