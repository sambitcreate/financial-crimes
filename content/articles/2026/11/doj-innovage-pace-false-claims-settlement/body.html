WASHINGTON — Denver-based senior-care operator InnovAge Holding Corporation has agreed to pay $36 million to resolve federal and state False Claims Act allegations that it collected monthly Medicare and Medicaid capitated payments while failing to provide timely medical services and specialist care to frail elderly enrollees.

Announced Nov. 17 by the Justice Department’s Civil Division, the agreement marks what prosecutors called a first-of-its-kind False Claims Act resolution involving a Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly (PACE) organization. Under PACE, providers receive fixed monthly per-patient payments from Medicare and Medicaid in exchange for managing all necessary medical and support services.

Federal and state officials alleged that between 2016 and 2022, InnovAge failed to arrange comprehensive medical care and timely specialist referrals—including in cardiology, neurology, oncology, and surgery—as rapidly as seniors’ clinical conditions required, even while continuing to draw monthly government payments.

Whistleblowers Triggered a Four-State Recovery

Under the settlement, roughly $25.7 million goes to the United States and approximately $10.3 million is divided among participating state Medicaid programs in Colorado, California, New Mexico, and Virginia. The government’s investigation grew out of two federal whistleblower suits filed under the qui tam provisions of the False Claims Act in the District of Colorado: United States ex rel. Poudyal v. InnovAge Holding Corp. (No. 1:21-cv-02464) and United States ex rel. Lapcewich v. Total Community Options Inc. (No. 1:22-cv-01686).

The Justice Department explicitly notes that the claims resolved are allegations only and that there has been no determination of liability. No company executives were criminally charged, leaving the resolution strictly civil with zero prison time imposed.

In a statement provided after publication, an InnovAge spokesperson said: “Over the past several years, we have worked closely with CMS and our state government partners while investing substantially to strengthen our clinical operations, compliance infrastructure and care delivery capabilities. Resolving these historical allegations from 2016 to 2022, with no admission of wrongdoing, allows us to move forward with a continued focus on delivering high-quality, compassionate care to the frail seniors we serve.”
