GURUGRAM, India — Public climate loans are supposed to put electric cabs on polluted roads. At Gensol Engineering and its ride-hailing affiliate BluSmart, regulators allege a slice of that state credit took a scenic detour straight into DLF The Camellias — Gurugram’s most coveted address.

Two state lenders, the Indian Renewable Energy Development Agency (IREDA) and Power Finance Corporation (PFC), extended ₹977.75 crore (roughly $114 million) in electric-vehicle fleet loans to Gensol. On July 30, 2025, the Securities and Exchange Board of India confirmed sweeping market and directorship bans against promoters Anmol and Puneet Jaggi.

Following the Green Rupee

SEBI’s confirmatory order says it traced ₹42.94 crore ($5 million) of those fleet-loan proceeds out through an EV dealer, Go-Auto, and onward via Capbridge Ventures, a related party, to pay for a luxury apartment at DLF The Camellias. The Jaggis have not been convicted of any offense.

By late 2026, the collapse has spread across several Indian forums: insolvency proceedings at the National Company Law Tribunal, appeals at the Securities Appellate Tribunal, and active probes by both the Enforcement Directorate and the Serious Fraud Investigation Office.

No criminal convictions have been entered, and the Enforcement Directorate and S.F.I.O. inquiries remain ongoing. Even so, the regulatory trail SEBI has laid out tells a tidy green-finance story: borrow ₹978 crore from state lenders to electrify urban transit, and — if SEBI’s findings hold — park $5 million of it in Gurugram’s priciest driveway.
