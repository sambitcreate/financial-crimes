JALANDHAR — India’s Directorate of Enforcement has provisionally attached 61 flats, residences, and commercial plots plus a bank account totaling ₹39.73 crore in its money-laundering investigation into the Vuenow Group, alleging the company collected ₹3,717 crore from retail investors for “Cloud Particle” data-centre units that never existed.

Disclosed Nov. 26 by the agency’s Jalandhar Zonal Office under Provisional Attachment Order (PAO) No. 16 of 2026, dated Nov. 17 under Section 5(1) of the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA), 2002, the order freezes ₹39.29 crore in immovable properties and ₹44.31 lakh in bank balances held in the names of M/s Vuenow Infotech Private Limited and associated individuals and entities.

Ten-Year Leases on Imaginary Racks

The PMLA inquiry stems from a First Information Report registered at Police Station Sector 58 in Gautam Budh Nagar (Noida), Uttar Pradesh, along with additional FIRs across other Indian states. At the center of the allegations is Vuenow Group’s “MyCloudParticle” offering, which pitched investors a sale-and-lease-back arrangement for fractional data-centre assets carrying promised monthly rentals for up to 120 months.

“The data-storage capacity presented to investors substantially exceeded the live/actual capacity of the servers and the funds amounting to ₹3,717 Crore was received from investors for Cloud Particles, which never existed with the company.” — Directorate of Enforcement, Jalandhar Zonal Office (Nov. 26, 2026)

According to investigators, Vuenow returned roughly ₹1,800 crore to early investors while diverting the remaining ₹1,917 crore (approximately $225.5 million), leaving later participants with neither monthly cloud-rental checks nor their principal.

Attachments Reach ₹236.95 Crore

Earlier in the investigation, the Enforcement Directorate arrested Sukhwinder Singh Kharour, Dimple Kharour, and Arif Nisar, issued two prior provisional attachment orders totaling ₹178.12 crore and ₹19.10 crore, and filed a prosecution complaint before the Special Court (PMLA) in Jalandhar on April 24, 2025.

With the latest 61-property sweep, cumulative provisional attachments in the Vuenow probe now stand at ₹236.95 crore — roughly 12.4 percent of the ₹1,917 crore net diversion alleged by investigators. Because the order is a provisional attachment and the prosecution remains pending before the Special Court, the accused retain the presumption of innocence.
