MUNICH — Cross-border European Union trade rules exempt shipments between Member States from value-added tax—a well-intentioned single-market feature that has turned compact consumer electronics into a favorite vehicle for tax carousels. On Nov. 19, the European Public Prosecutor’s Office in Munich announced the arrest of three suspects across Bulgaria, the Netherlands, and Poland over an alleged AirPods V.A.T. loop estimated to have cost Germany €23 million ($25 million).

Coordinated raids, searches, and seizures took place across all three nations at the request of E.P.P.O. Munich, backed by Berlin’s tax office (Finanzamt Berlin), the Netherlands’ Fiscal Information and Investigation Service (F.I.O.D.), Poland’s Central Anti-Corruption Bureau regional office in Bydgoszcz, and Bulgaria’s National Investigative Service and General Directorate National Police.

Missing Traders Inside the €195 Million ‘Midas’ Cluster

Prosecutors suspect the detainees operated through chains of companies and “missing traders” registered in Germany and Bulgaria to buy and resell AirPods across internal E.U. borders while pocketing V.A.T. owed to national treasuries. According to the E.P.P.O., the activities of these three suspects alone may have generated €23 million in estimated German tax losses.

The arrests form part of the wider Investigation Midas probe, which targets a transnational syndicate suspected of running a €195 million V.A.T. carousel spanning smartphones, small electronic devices, and protective face masks. As the E.P.P.O. emphasized in its Nov. 19 release, all persons concerned are presumed innocent until proven otherwise in competent courts of law.
