CENTRAL ISLIP, N.Y. — A West Hempstead father and son who operated a purported Long Island credit-repair storefront called Credit Reset have pleaded guilty in federal court to running a pandemic tax-credit assembly line that billed the IRS for more than $600 million across over 8,000 false returns.

According to the Justice Department’s Nov. 24 Office of Public Affairs announcement, Keith Williams, 48, pleaded guilty on Nov. 24 to one count of conspiracy to defraud the United States and one count of wire fraud. His son, Jamari Lewis, 28, pleaded guilty on Nov. 19 to one count of wire fraud. Both defendants agreed to pay $46,756,084.89 in restitution to the IRS, matching the actual Treasury payouts generated by their filings.

Double-Dipping Payroll Credits at Scale

Court filings show that Williams, Lewis, and recruited co-conspirators acted as tax preparers out of Credit Reset, repeatedly targeting two Congress-authorized pandemic relief mechanisms: the Employee Retention Credit (ERC) and the paid sick and family leave credit. Prosecutors said each fraudulent employment tax return violated basic federal eligibility math—claiming sick and family leave credits exceeding the total wages reported on the return, counting identical wages simultaneously as both sick leave and family leave, or double-dipping the same wages for both leave credits and the ERC.

“As admitted in court, Keith Williams and his son Jamari Lewis used a purported credit repair business to file more than 8,000 false tax returns seeking over $600 million in fraudulent COVID-19 tax credits.” — Assistant Attorney General Colin M. McDonald, National Fraud Enforcement Division

The operators collected U.S. Treasury refund checks directly and charged clients a fee or percentage cut of each refund received, while paying recruited co-conspirators a share of the incoming Treasury checks. Alongside the $46.76 million restitution obligation, Williams agreed to forfeit $4,848,998.44 in proceeds and Lewis agreed to forfeit $671,511.11.

Two Co-Conspirators Already Pleaded

Williams faces a statutory maximum of five years in prison on the conspiracy count and 30 years on the wire-fraud count at a sentencing date to be scheduled; Lewis faces up to 30 years in prison and is set for sentencing on April 19, 2027. Two co-conspirators previously entered guilty pleas in the Eastern District of New York case: Janine Davis pleaded guilty to wire fraud in May, and Tiffany Williams was sentenced in May to 36 months in federal prison. The case was investigated by IRS Criminal Investigation, the U.S. Postal Inspection Service, and Homeland Security Investigations.
