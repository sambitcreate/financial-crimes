LONDON — Seven years after accepting corporate bribery charges under a Deferred Prosecution Agreement—and two years after prosecutors notified a judge that the company had never paid the bill—British seismology manufacturer Güralp Systems Limited walked out of Southwark Crown Court on Nov. 20 with a fresh installment schedule and its corporate criminal immunity still intact.

Under the court-approved DPA variation announced by the U.K. Serious Fraud Office, Güralp Systems must pay £2.5 million (approximately $3.17 million) to taxpayers, covering its unpaid £2,069,861 disgorgement from October 2019 plus up to £400,000 in accrued interest and S.F.O. legal costs. A first installment of £400,000 is due on Dec. 1, 2026, followed by an agreed schedule for the remaining balance.

The original October 2019 agreement resolved an S.F.O. investigation into corrupt payments made to secure contracts with a South Korean public seismic research institute. Under that deal, Güralp Systems accepted corporate charges of conspiracy to make corrupt payments and failure to prevent bribery, and agreed to disgorge £2,069,861 in gross profits—with no additional punitive financial penalty imposed at the time—over a five-year term. In December 2019, three former executives prosecuted separately at Southwark Crown Court—Dr. Cansun Güralp, Andrew Bell, and Natalie Pearce—were acquitted by a jury.

Five Years Unpaid, Two Years in the High Court

When the five-year payment window closed without the disgorgement being paid, the S.F.O. notified Southwark Crown Court on Nov. 21, 2024, that it believed the company had breached the DPA. Rather than quietly writing a check, Güralp Systems litigated whether prosecutors could still enforce the agreement. On Jan. 13, 2026, the High Court ruled that the DPA remained enforceable by the S.F.O., and on Feb. 10, 2026, refused the company permission to appeal to the Supreme Court.

To discourage a repeat performance, the Nov. 20 variation adds binding clauses barring Güralp Systems from removing company assets or intellectual property, and warns that any further failure to comply will terminate the agreement and trigger criminal prosecution. “Companies that enter into a Deferred Prosecution Agreement must comply with its terms, or we will terminate the agreement and seek a prosecution,” said Jason Williams, Head of Division at the S.F.O., in the agency’s Nov. 20 press release.
