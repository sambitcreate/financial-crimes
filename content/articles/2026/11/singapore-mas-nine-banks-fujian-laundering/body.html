SINGAPORE — After S$3 billion ($2.25 billion) in dirty cash tied to the “Fujian gang” flowed through Singapore’s wealth sector, the Monetary Authority of Singapore sent nine marquee banks the bill on July 4, 2025: S$27.45 million ($21.5 million) combined, or 0.915 percent of the loot.

The sanctioned roster reads like a private-banking directory — Credit Suisse, UOB, UBS, Citi, Julius Baer, LGT, and three peers. No bank executives went to jail. Dirty money, it turns out, rarely bothers with back-alley shell shops when marble wealth-management lobbies are open.

Less Than a Penny on the Dollar

In private banking, 91.5 basis points is a modest annual management fee. Here, it was the collective regulatory surcharge for letting S$3 billion clear customer checks and transaction alerts: less than 92 Singapore cents for every S$100 laundered.

MAS defended the finalized orders as proportionate to the specific anti-money-laundering lapses identified at each firm. Global watchdogs were less impressed. Throughout 2025 and into 2026, the Financial Action Task Force criticized the penalties as too small to deter anyone.

At Singapore’s institutional bank rate, laundering $1,800 would cost you $16.47 and zero nights in a cell. The July 2025 orders are legally closed, leaving behind a ratio that reads less like enforcement than an affordable clearing fee.
