SINGAPORE — After police seized or froze more than S$3 billion ($2.25 billion) in assets in the “Fujian gang” laundering case, the Monetary Authority of Singapore sent nine marquee banks the bill on July 4, 2025: S$27.45 million ($21.5 million) combined for anti-money-laundering lapses, or 0.915 percent of the seized haul.

The sanctioned roster reads like a private-banking directory — Credit Suisse, UOB, UBS, Citi, Julius Baer, LGT, and three peers. No bank executives went to jail. MAS found all nine failed to adequately probe red flags in some customers’ claimed sources of wealth — a reminder that suspect money rarely needs back-alley shell shops when marble wealth-management lobbies are open.

Less Than a Penny on the Dollar

In private banking, 91.5 basis points is a modest annual management fee. Here, it was the collective regulatory surcharge for the customer-check and transaction-monitoring lapses MAS identified: less than 92 Singapore cents for every S$100 police seized in the case.

MAS defended the finalized orders as proportionate to the specific anti-money-laundering lapses identified at each firm. Global watchdogs were less impressed. Throughout 2025 and into 2026, the Financial Action Task Force criticized the penalties as too small to deter anyone.

At that rate, waving through $1,800 in suspect cash would cost you $16.47 and zero nights in a cell. The July 2025 orders are legally closed, leaving behind a ratio that reads less like enforcement than an affordable clearing fee.
