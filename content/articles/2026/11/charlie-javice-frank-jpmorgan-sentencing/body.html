NEW YORK — Few invoices in Wall Street history delivered better short-term leverage than the $18,000 Charlie Javice paid a data scientist to invent four million college students. It persuaded JPMorgan Chase to pay $175 million for her startup, Frank — and earned Ms. Javice 85 months in federal prison on Sept. 29, 2025.

Judge Alvin Hellerstein imposed the seven-year, one-month sentence in the Southern District of New York six months after a federal jury convicted Ms. Javice and co-defendant Olivier Amar of defrauding America’s largest bank, alongside restitution and forfeiture orders.

$43.75 per Imaginary Student

JPMorgan thought it was buying a pipeline to 4.25 million college students. Trial evidence showed Frank’s actual user count stood below 300,000 — and when JPMorgan finally sent a test email campaign to 400,000 rows after closing, more than 70 percent bounced as nonexistent addresses.

The verdict leaves behind an awkward monument to corporate due diligence: armies of M&A bankers and lawyers blessed a $175 million acquisition priced at roughly $43.75 per synthetic spreadsheet row before anyone thought to send a single test email.

Still, the criminal docket ended without a Wall Street discount. Unlike banks that settle laundering lapses with civil checks, Ms. Javice went through a Manhattan jury trial and drew 85 months once her four million students failed to answer roll call.
