NEW YORK — Charlie Javice, the founder of Frank, was sentenced on Sept. 29, 2025, in Manhattan federal court to 85 months in prison for defrauding JPMorgan Chase in its $175 million acquisition of her company — a corporate buyout anchored by an $18,000 spreadsheet listing four million fabricated college student accounts.

The sentence, handed down by Judge Hellerstein in the United States District Court for the Southern District of New York, followed the March 2025 criminal convictions of both Ms. Javice and her co-defendant, Olivier Amar. For one of Wall Street’s most sophisticated banking institutions, the trial and September sentencing closed an unusually embarrassing chapter of mergers-and-acquisitions due diligence.

Seldom in corporate finance has a modest data-fabrication invoice yielded such extraordinary short-term leverage. An $18,000 outlay to manufacture four million imaginary student rows helped persuade JPMorgan Chase to pay $175 million for Frank — a valuation that effectively priced each phantom college student at roughly $43.75 before federal prosecutors intervened.

Four Million Imaginary Customers

When JPMorgan Chase moved to acquire Frank for $175 million, the bank believed it was buying a rapidly scaling pipeline to millions of college students. At trial in Manhattan federal court, prosecutors proved that the user roster presented during acquisition talks had been massively inflated through a fabricated database of four million nonexistent student accounts procured for $18,000.

In March 2025, a federal jury convicted both Ms. Javice and Mr. Amar of defrauding the bank in connection with the sale. The verdict underscored a paradox familiar to veterans of Silicon Valley and Wall Street alike: billion-dollar banks routinely deploy armies of lawyers, investment bankers and compliance specialists to scrutinize acquisition targets, yet a convincing spreadsheet of fabricated customer rows still cleared the deal table before the discrepancies were exposed.

Following the March 2025 convictions of Charlie Javice and Olivier Amar in the Southern District of New York, Judge Hellerstein sentenced Ms. Javice on Sept. 29, 2025, to 85 months in federal prison over the $175 million JPMorgan Chase acquisition of Frank.

Seven Years and One Month for a Spreadsheet

Judge Hellerstein’s Sept. 29, 2025 sentence of 85 months — seven years and one month in federal prison — drew a firm criminal boundary around startup fundraising and M&A exaggeration. Unlike civil securities disputes that end quietly in corporate settlements without admissions of wrongdoing, the Frank prosecution proceeded through a full criminal jury trial to felony convictions and multi-year incarceration.

For corporate dealmakers, the Southern District of New York docket offered a sobering ratio that requires no financial modeling to grasp: $18,000 spent on fabricated student rows, $175 million paid by America’s largest bank, and 85 months in federal prison once the imaginary students failed to answer roll call.
