SYRACUSE, ITALY — Grants meant to build “innovative and sustainable school laboratories” and remove architectural barriers across high schools in Sicily ended up funding missing doors, absent lighting, and a suspected 15% kickback split, according to precautionary court orders announced by the European Public Prosecutor’s Office on Nov. 24.

Acting on a request from EPPO’s Palermo office, the Judge for Preliminary Investigations (GIP) of the Court of Syracuse barred a director of general and administrative services (DSGA) serving two secondary schools and five businessmen from holding public office. Officers of Italy’s Guardia di Finanza served the precautionary bans alongside a court-ordered seizure of €33,500 in assets belonging to the school director, matching the alleged proceeds traced to the official in the Nov. 24 EPPO release.

A 5% / 10% Split on Staged Tenders

The €1.5 million upgrade program drew financing from both the European Regional Development Fund (ERDF) and Italy’s National Recovery and Resilience Plan (NRRP). Prosecutors allege the school administrative director colluded with a businessman from Modica to pre-select favored contractors and stage competitive tenders using other firms solely as window dressing to simulate legitimate bidding.

In exchange for winning the contracts, investigators allege, selected suppliers paid a 15% kickback on the amounts invoiced to the schools—split 5% to the school director and 10% to the Modica intermediary who managed contractor relationships. A school principal working at both schools is also under scrutiny for allegedly making false statements in accounting records and administrative paperwork covering the work.

“In other cases, certain works had not been carried out at all, including lighting, painting and gate automation, as well as the supply of doors.” — European Public Prosecutor’s Office, Nov. 24, 2026

Investigators reported that even where work took place, deliverables repeatedly fell below tender specifications. The suspects face formal corruption charges, with the administrative director additionally charged with aggravated fraud to obtain public funds, while the principal is slated for questioning over alleged false public-document entries. EPPO emphasized that all persons named in the investigation are presumed innocent until proven otherwise in competent Italian courts.
