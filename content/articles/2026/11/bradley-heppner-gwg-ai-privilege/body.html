NEW YORK — Bradley Heppner stole more than $150 million from retail bondholders in a bankrupt public company, got convicted on all counts on May 7, 2026, and along the way established an expensive piece of legal precedent: an A.I. chatbot has not passed the bar.

In docket 25-Cr.-503 in the Southern District of New York, prosecutors proved that the former GWG Holdings chairman and Beneficient chief executive siphoned $150 million from ordinary holders of GWG L-Bonds into HCLP, a shell entity tied to himself, leaving retail investors holding bankruptcy wreckage. Sentencing before Judge Jed Rakoff is pending.

Twenty Dollars a Month, Zero Privilege

The trial’s lasting footnote came when Mr. Heppner’s defense argued that prompts he typed into the commercial chatbot Claude were shielded by attorney-client privilege. Judge Rakoff rejected the claim.

White-collar defense lawyers in Manhattan bill $1,800 an hour for one primary reason: the law forbids prosecutors from reading their notes. A consumer chatbot subscription is cheaper and replies faster, but it is neither a licensed lawyer nor a confidential retainer — making Mr. Heppner’s prompts ordinary discoverable evidence.

For executives nationwide, docket 25-Cr.-503 delivers two lessons at once: looting $150 million from retail bondholders into a private shell is still a federal felony, and brainstorming your defense with a third-party chatbot is a remarkably thorough way to type the prosecution’s exhibits yourself.
