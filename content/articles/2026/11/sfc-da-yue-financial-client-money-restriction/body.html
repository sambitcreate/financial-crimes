HONG KONG — Segregated client accounts exist for one elementary reason: one trader’s blown-up futures margin call is not supposed to be paid out of another customer’s cash balance. On Nov. 23, Hong Kong’s Securities and Futures Commission (S.F.C.) stepped in to freeze DA YUE Financial Group Limited after uncovering an estimated HK$29.6 million ($3.8 million) client-money shortfall.

Issued under sections 204 and 205 of Hong Kong’s Securities and Futures Ordinance, the restriction notice bars DA YUE Financial Group—a brokerage licensed for Type 2 (dealing in futures contracts) and Type 5 (advising on futures contracts) regulated activities—from carrying on any regulated business or disposing of or dealing with property held on behalf of clients without the regulator’s prior written consent. According to the S.F.C.’s Nov. 23 enforcement notice (26PR197), roughly 270 active clients currently hold credit balances at the firm.

Covering Margin Losses With Other Customers’ Cash

The regulator’s initial inquiries indicated not only that DA YUE faces a HK$29.6 million shortfall in client funds, but also that the brokerage lacks sufficient resources to plug the hole itself. The S.F.C. stated that it believes the firm used client money to satisfy margin requirements and cover futures trading losses incurred by certain other clients.

In unusually blunt statutory language, the commission said those findings raise serious doubts about DA YUE’s “reliability, integrity and ability to carry on its regulated activities competently, honestly and fairly,” calling into question whether the brokerage remains fit and proper to stay licensed in Hong Kong. Narrow carve-outs in the order allow deposits into authorized bank accounts and ordinary-course operational expenses only with prior written S.F.C. sign-off.

The commission noted that its review remains ongoing and that the restriction notice is an interim protective measure designed to preserve remaining client assets while investigators assess the full extent of the deficit.
