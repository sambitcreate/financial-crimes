NEW YORK — In a white-collar docket frequently populated by deferred prosecution agreements and zero-prison consent orders, a Manhattan federal judge drew a sharp line on Nov. 20: 20 years in federal prison and $158.4 million in combined financial judgments for the organizer of an international fraud network that drained seniors’ retirement savings in cash and gold bars.

U.S. District Judge Mary Kay Vyskocil sentenced Jingbin Jiang to 240 months in prison, followed by three years of supervised release, after Jiang pleaded guilty on April 7, 2026, to leading an international elder fraud conspiracy that stole well over $100 million from hundreds of elderly victims across the United States. Alongside the custodial term, Judge Vyskocil ordered Jiang to pay $148,182,727.47 in restitution, forfeit $10,162,729, and pay a $50,000 fine.

Pop-Up Alerts, Impersonated Agents, and Bullion Couriers

According to the U.S. Attorney’s Office for the Southern District of New York, Jiang’s operation relied on bogus computer security pop-up alerts and overseas call centers coordinated across countries including India and China. Callers posed as technical support personnel or federal government agents, convincing frightened seniors that their bank accounts or identities had been compromised—and threatening arrest or financial ruin unless they liquidated their holdings.

Victims were instructed to withdraw large sums of cash or convert their savings into physical gold bars, which were then picked up directly by couriers working for the network. The sentencing, detailed in the Nov. 20 S.D.N.Y. announcement, stands out on this broadsheet’s ledger as a rare case where both custodial time and restitution orders matched the scale of the verified loss.
