HONG KONG — Fourteen years after auditors first flagged hundreds of millions of renminbi in phantom cash on the balance sheet of China Longevity Group Company Limited, Hong Kong’s Court of First Instance has disqualified three of its former executive directors from running corporations—without imposing a monetary fine or a single day in prison.

Announced Nov. 26 by Hong Kong’s Securities and Futures Commission (SFC, Release 26PR200), the civil orders under section 214 of the Securities and Futures Ordinance bar former chairman and executive director Lin Shengxiong and former executive director Zhang Hongwang for 10 years each, and former executive director Huang Wanneng for four years. All three were ordered to pay the regulator’s legal costs.

Nearly One-Fifth of Net Assets Overstated

An SFC investigation determined that China Longevity—formerly known as Sijia Group Company Limited (Stock Code: 1863)—overstated its cash and cash equivalents by roughly RMB 198.9 million as of Dec. 31, 2011, and by RMB 302.4 million as of June 30, 2012. Those figures represented approximately 13.6% and 19.9% of the listed company’s reported net assets on the respective dates, rendering its 2011 and 2012 annual reports and 2012 interim report materially misleading.

The company’s then-auditors first uncovered the cash overstatements and reported them to Lin, Zhang, and Huang between late August and early September 2012, before surfacing additional irregularities between November and December 2012. According to the SFC, Lin and Zhang exercised close control over the bank account holding most of the company’s cash yet failed to prevent the misstatements, and all three directors failed to procure timely, accurate public disclosure.

Twelve Years Suspended, Resolved by Carecraft

Trading in China Longevity’s shares has been suspended at the SFC’s direction since Dec. 4, 2014—nearly twelve full years before the final director bans landed. The SFC initiated section 214 proceedings on Nov. 25, 2022, and the Court disposed of the matter via Hong Kong’s Carecraft procedure, entering agreed orders on an agreed statement of facts.

Under the court’s decree, Lin, Zhang, and Huang are barred from serving as directors, liquidators, receivers, or managers—or taking part directly or indirectly in managing any corporation carrying on business in Hong Kong—for the duration of their bans, closing a decade-plus corporate governance saga with zero criminal sanctions and HK$0 in pecuniary penalties.
