LONDON — Nearly seven years after the failure of London Capital & Finance Plc erased an estimated £251 million in retail savings, the United Kingdom’s Serious Fraud Office has brought criminal charges against eight individuals tied to the Tunbridge Wells mini-bond issuer.

In an enforcement announcement published Nov. 16, the Serious Fraud Office said former chief executive Michael Andrew Thomson, 53, along with Simon Hume-Kendall, 73; Elten Barker, 53; Paul Careless, 51; Robert Sedgwick, 78; and John Russell-Murphy, 52, have each been charged with conspiracy to defraud. Prosecutors also charged Helen Hume-Kendall, 64, with concealing criminal property.

An eighth defendant faces charges of conspiracy to defraud and acting in contravention of a director disqualification undertaking. The agency noted that the eighth individual’s legal team is expected to apply for anonymity at the initial hearing.

A Seven-Year Road to Westminster

London Capital & Finance collapsed into administration in January 2019 after marketing fixed-rate mini-bonds to more than 11,200 retail savers across Britain. The failure became one of the country’s most closely scrutinized retail investment collapses, prompting regulatory reviews and a taxpayer-backed compensation scheme.

All eight defendants are scheduled to make their first appearance at Westminster Magistrates’ Court on Dec. 7, 2026. The criminal charges represent allegations only; each defendant is presumed innocent unless and until proven guilty in court.

Because the prosecution has not yet reached trial or sentencing, The Financial Crimes records the case’s financial recovery and custody outcomes as pending.
