REWARI, India — Under India’s Health Security se National Security (H.S.N.S.) Cess Act, 2025, tax inspectors do not have to count individual packets of pan masala to calculate what a factory owes—they simply count the installed packing machines. That capacity-based math produced a ₹116.48 crore ($13.8 million) bill after Central G.S.T. officers raided an unregistered facility in Haryana.

Acting on specific intelligence, the C.G.S.T. Faridabad Commissionerate searched a unit on Ram Garh Road in Rewari on Nov. 16, according to a Nov. 20 Ministry of Finance release issued through the Press Information Bureau. Inside, officers discovered four vertical pouch-packing machines producing “Dhanraj” brand pan masala.

Four Machines, Zero Registration

Investigators found that the operator had neither obtained registration under the H.S.N.S. Cess Act—which took effect on Feb. 1, 2026—nor declared the four packing machines as mandated by statute. Because the levy assesses pan masala manufacturers strictly on installed machine capacity, authorities calculated the unpaid and allegedly evaded statutory cess at ₹116.48 crore.

Officers seized all four machines and arrested the person who owned and operated them. The accused was produced before the jurisdictional court and remanded to 14 days of judicial custody while further investigation continues. As in all pre-trial enforcement proceedings, the allegations remain subject to adjudication and the accused is presumed innocent.
