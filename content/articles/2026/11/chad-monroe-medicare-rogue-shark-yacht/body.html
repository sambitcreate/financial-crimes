TAMPA, Fla. — Subtlety was never part of Chad Monroe’s business plan. On Nov. 13, 2026, the Maksix Medical principal pleaded guilty in the Middle District of Florida to a $42 million Medicare billing scheme — after federal agents seized his 47-foot yacht, christened “The Rogue Shark.”

Mr. Monroe admitted billing Medicare for medically unnecessary orthopedic braces and genetic tests, two reimbursement lines prized by fraudsters because automated payouts pile into eight figures long before audits catch up. Sentencing is pending.

A $21.1 Billion Two-Year Tab

His plea landed months after the Justice Department’s June 23, 2026 National Health Care Fraud Takedown, which charged 455 defendants across 56 federal districts in alleged schemes exceeding $6.5 billion. Add the 2025 sweep, and federal prosecutors have logged $21.1 billion in alleged health care fraud in just two years. Defendants in the broader sweep who have not pleaded guilty remain presumed innocent.

Towing “The Rogue Shark” into federal custody gives prosecutors an irresistible trophy photo. Still, a $21.1 billion two-year billing tab across 56 districts suggests that for every yacht owner who names his boat after a federal exhibit, dozens of quieter operators are still billing Medicare with far better branding.
