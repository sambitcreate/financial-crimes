ATLANTA — More than 800 online investors wired $62.8 million through CrowdStreet thinking they were buying an Atlanta office tower. Instead, they bought Elie Schwartz luxury wristwatches and stock-market margin calls.

In May 2025, a federal judge in the Northern District of Georgia sentenced the former Nightingale Properties chief executive to 87 months in prison and ordered more than $45 million in restitution after his guilty plea, alongside a parallel S.E.C. civil action.

The Missing Skyscraper

Real-estate crowdfunding platforms promised to let ordinary investors buy into institutional commercial property deals once locked behind private-equity doors. The Nightingale offering drew $62.8 million from more than 800 CrowdStreet accounts on the promise that the money would sit in deal-specific escrow until closing.

Mr. Schwartz admitted in court that he drained those accounts anyway, routing capital into high-end watches, personal spending, and speculative brokerage margin trades. When the tower closing never happened, the escrow was already empty.

Seven years and three months in federal custody is real accountability — though a $45 million restitution order is far easier for a judge to sign than for investors to collect once their building has evaporated into brokerage losses. Crowdfunding democratized commercial real estate; it just forgot to lock the sponsor out of the cash drawer.
