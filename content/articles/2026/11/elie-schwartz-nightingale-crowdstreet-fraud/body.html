ATLANTA — Elie Schwartz, the former chief executive of New York-based Nightingale Properties, was sentenced in May 2025 in Atlanta federal court to 87 months in prison and ordered to pay more than $45 million in restitution after pleading guilty to diverting $62.8 million raised from more than 800 online real-estate investors into luxury watches and stock-market margin trades.

The sentencing in the United States District Court for the Northern District of Georgia, paired with parallel civil enforcement action by the Securities and Exchange Commission, closed the criminal adjudication of one of the starkest cautionary episodes in retail commercial real-estate crowdfunding. More than 800 individual investors had committed $62.8 million through the CrowdStreet platform under the belief that their money was earmarked to acquire an Atlanta office tower.

Instead of closing on the promised commercial property, federal prosecutors established — and Mr. Schwartz admitted in his guilty plea — that the capital was siphoned out of investor accounts to finance personal indulgences and speculative securities trading.

An Office Tower Replaced by Wristwatches and Margin Calls

Real-estate crowdfunding platforms such as CrowdStreet were built to widen access to institutional-scale commercial property deals, allowing individual investors to pool millions of dollars into single-asset acquisitions once reserved for private equity funds. In the Nightingale Properties offering, more than 800 investors responded by wiring $62.8 million toward the Atlanta office tower transaction.

The gap between the pitch deck and the bank statements proved absolute. Rather than holding investor subscriptions strictly for the Atlanta acquisition, Mr. Schwartz diverted the proceeds toward high-end luxury watches and leveraged margin trades in the equity markets. When the tower acquisition failed to materialize and investors sought answers, the promised escrow had already been drained.

Following his guilty plea in the Northern District of Georgia alongside parallel S.E.C. civil enforcement, Elie Schwartz was sentenced in May 2025 to 87 months in federal prison and ordered to pay more than $45 million in restitution over the $62.8 million CrowdStreet offering.

Criminal Prison Time and a Nine-Figure Cautionary Ledger

Unlike corporate regulatory settlements resolved exclusively through civil penalties, Mr. Schwartz’s guilty plea carried both felony incarceration and substantial financial forfeiture. His May 2025 sentence of 87 months — seven years and three months in federal custody — alongside an order to pay more than $45 million in restitution reflected the breadth of the harm inflicted on more than 800 retail participants, while the S.E.C.’s parallel civil action addressed federal securities-law violations.

Still, restitution orders are easier to enter on a judgment sheet than to collect once diverted capital has evaporated in margin accounts. For the hundreds of CrowdStreet investors who thought they were buying a share of an Atlanta office tower, the Northern District of Georgia docket left behind a blunt lesson in modern digital fundraising: an online investment portal can democratize access to commercial real estate, but it cannot prevent a sponsor from treating an acquisition fund like a personal brokerage account.
