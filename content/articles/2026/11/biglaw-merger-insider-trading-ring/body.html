BOSTON — Corporations pay elite law firms thousands of dollars an hour for code names, locked deal rooms, and airtight secrecy. According to federal filings unsealed in May 2026, a 30-person insider-trading ring quietly ran a decade-long subscription service straight out of those files.

Prosecutors in the District of Massachusetts and the S.E.C. charged Nicolo Nourafchan, Robert Yadgarov, and 28 others with trading on leaked BigLaw merger documents ahead of roughly 30 acquisitions worth more than $140 billion combined — including deals involving Anadarko, Express Scripts, and iRobot — for tens of millions in alleged profits.

At the unsealing, 19 defendants were arrested, two remained fugitives abroad, and one had already pleaded guilty. The other 29 defendants are presumed innocent unless proven guilty in court.

Ten Years of Unbilled Advisory

Government complaints describe a durable wholesale chain rather than a single rogue associate. Nonpublic merger timelines allegedly flowed from insiders inside major corporate law practices through tippers and middlemen to a wider ring of traders placing pre-announcement equity and options bets.

The parallel dockets split the risk cleanly: Boston prosecutors are seeking felony convictions and prison time, while the S.E.C. wants disgorgement, interest, and civil fines under a lower burden of proof.

Boards spend small fortunes insulating takeover talks from rival bidders and analysts. If prosecutors prove their case, the most reliable leak ran right through the confidential binders billed at $1,500 an hour to keep the secret.
