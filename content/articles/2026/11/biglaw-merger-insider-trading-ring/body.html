BOSTON — Federal prosecutors in Massachusetts and the Securities and Exchange Commission unsealed coordinated criminal and civil enforcement actions in May 2026 charging 30 individuals in a decade-long global insider-trading ring accused of turning confidential merger files from major corporate law firms into a private trading pipeline.

According to filings in the United States District Court for the District of Massachusetts and parallel civil complaints brought by the S.E.C., the network — whose charged participants include Nicolo Nourafchan, Robert Yadgarov and 28 other defendants — allegedly generated tens of millions of dollars in illicit profits across roughly 30 major corporate acquisitions. Together, the targeted transactions represented more than $140 billion in combined deal value, spanning marquee corporate combinations involving Anadarko, Express Scripts and iRobot.

When the enforcement sweep was unsealed in May 2026, authorities reported 19 arrests, two defendants remaining fugitives abroad and one guilty plea already entered. For the 29 defendants who have not pleaded guilty, the criminal indictments and civil S.E.C. complaints remain allegations, and each accused individual is presumed innocent unless and until proven guilty.

An Informal Supply Chain Inside Elite Law Firms

Major mergers and acquisitions depend on an elaborate ritual of confidentiality: code names, restricted document rooms and thousands of billable hours spent drafting nondisclosure agreements. According to prosecutors and securities regulators, the alleged network bypassed those safeguards with striking durability, operating across roughly ten years and drawing material nonpublic information from confidential files housed inside elite “BigLaw” practices.

Government filings depict a layered distribution chain rather than a lone rogue employee. Confidential deal intelligence allegedly moved from insiders with access to law-firm merger records through tippers and intermediaries to a wider circle of traders positioned to place timely bets ahead of public merger announcements. In deals measured in the tens of billions of dollars — including corporate combinations involving Anadarko, Express Scripts and iRobot — advance knowledge of an impending bid offered near-certain windfalls.

Parallel criminal filings in the District of Massachusetts and civil complaints by the S.E.C. allege that confidential merger-and-acquisition records from major law firms were systematically relayed through intermediaries to 30 traders across approximately 30 transactions exceeding $140 billion in combined value.

Parallel Criminal and Civil Dockets

The dual-track enforcement approach separates the potential consequences facing the accused. In Boston federal court, criminal prosecutors seek felony convictions and prison sentences under federal securities-fraud and conspiracy statutes. Simultaneously, the S.E.C.’s civil enforcement action pursues disgorgement of alleged illicit profits, prejudgment interest and civil monetary penalties — remedies that operate under a lower civil burden of proof and are frequently resolved on separate timelines.

With 19 defendants arrested, two fugitives sought overseas and one guilty plea already on the record, the case illustrates a recurring vulnerability at the center of global dealmaking. Corporations routinely spend fortunes insulating merger negotiations from rival bidders and Wall Street analysts, only to discover — if prosecutors prove their allegations — that the quietest leak ran straight through the confidential binders meant to protect the deal.
