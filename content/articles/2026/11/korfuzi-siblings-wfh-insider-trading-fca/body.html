LONDON — Remote work was widely celebrated in the City of London for freeing financial professionals from the morning commute and the watchful gaze of open-plan trading floors. In the case of Redinel Korfuzi, a former research analyst at Janus Henderson, and his sister, Oerta Korfuzi, working from home also supplied a rarer workplace convenience: the ability to pivot from a confidential corporate email to a leveraged market bet in a matter of minutes.

That domestic arrangement has now produced a landmark criminal sanction in Britain. Convicted in June 2025 following a prosecution by the Financial Conduct Authority, the Korfuzi siblings were sentenced at Southwark Crown Court on July 4, 2025, to a combined 11 years in prison — a record sentence for an FCA insider-trading prosecution. In 2026, authorities have pursued Proceeds of Crime Act confiscation proceedings aimed at clawing back the illicit proceeds of the scheme.

At trial, prosecutors laid out how the pair generated roughly £1 million, or about $1.25 million, in unlawful profits by exploiting confidential market-moving information obtained through Redinel Korfuzi's employment as an analyst at Janus Henderson. Rather than relying solely on digital accounts to conceal the windfall, the siblings paired modern remote-work trading with an old-fashioned cash-handling network stretching from a Knightsbridge vault to Albania.

Four Minutes From Inbox to Execution

The Financial Conduct Authority's forensic timeline stripped away much of the mystery traditionally surrounding insider-dealing cases. According to the prosecution's evidence at Southwark Crown Court, privileged corporate information flowing into Mr. Korfuzi's home workspace was followed within minutes — at times as quickly as four minutes — by leveraged trades designed to capture sharp price movements before the wider investing public had any opportunity to react.

Following the June 2025 convictions at Southwark Crown Court, the UK Financial Conduct Authority noted that the Korfuzi siblings' combined 11-year prison sentence set a record for an FCA insider-trading prosecution, reflecting both the abuse of confidential analyst access while working from home and the accompanying money-laundering operation.

What elevated the prosecution beyond a routine breach of brokerage compliance was the sheer volume of physical cash movement attached to the profits. Prosecutors detailed 173 separate cash deposits used to obscure and transfer funds, linking the trading gains to money laundering activity involving a Knightsbridge safety deposit vault and transfers routed toward Albania. The methodical cadence of those deposits contradicted any suggestion of impulsive opportunism.

By the Numbers

  • ~£1 million (~$1.25M): Illicit insider-trading profits established in the UK FCA prosecution.
  • 11 combined years: Record total prison sentence handed down at Southwark Crown Court on July 4, 2025.
  • 173 cash deposits: Number of cash transactions linked to laundering funds via a Knightsbridge vault and Albania.
  • 2026 POCA proceedings: Active Proceeds of Crime Act confiscation process following the June 2025 convictions.

The End of the Unsupervised Desk

For Britain's financial regulator, which has long faced criticism that insider dealing in London too often ended in quiet settlements or acquittals, the Korfuzi verdict served as an unmistakable demonstration of deterrence. Combining insider trading charges with money-laundering convictions allowed prosecutors to secure a custodial benchmark rarely seen in City white-collar dockets.

At the same time, the case has prompted awkward conversations inside London asset managers about the compliance perimeter of the hybrid workplace. A corporate firewall can restrict downloads and log keystrokes, yet it struggles to police what happens four minutes after an analyst reads an email across a kitchen table — at least until the Financial Conduct Authority arrives to read the calendar back in court.
