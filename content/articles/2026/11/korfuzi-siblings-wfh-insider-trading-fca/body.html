LONDON — Remote work spared City of London financiers the morning Tube and open-plan trading floors. For former Janus Henderson research analyst Redinel Korfuzi and his sister, Oerta, it offered an extra perk: four minutes between opening a confidential corporate email and placing a leveraged bet.

Convicted at Southwark Crown Court in June 2025 after a Financial Conduct Authority prosecution, the siblings were sentenced on July 4, 2025, to a combined 11 years in prison — a record for an F.C.A. insider-dealing case. Proceeds of Crime Act confiscation hearings remain underway in 2026.

Four Minutes From Inbox to Execution

Prosecutors proved the pair netted roughly £1 million ($1.25 million) trading ahead of nonpublic announcements flowing onto Mr. Korfuzi’s home screen, sometimes executing leveraged bets within four minutes of receiving confidential emails.

Then came the analog laundry. Prosecutors traced 173 separate cash deposits routed through a Knightsbridge safety-deposit vault and onward toward Albania, adding money-laundering convictions that pushed the custody term to an F.C.A. high-water mark.

The math makes for an uncomfortable comparison across London’s financial pages: banks that help move billions pay rounding-error fines, while a kitchen-table analyst who stole £1 million gets eleven combined years when the F.C.A. reads his email timestamps back in court.
