GURUGRAM — India’s Directorate of Enforcement has provisionally attached ₹545.96 crore (roughly $64.2 million) in real estate tied to Vatika Limited and its promoters Anil Bhalla and Gautam Bhalla, including a farmhouse in New Delhi’s Sultanpur Farms and assets held inside two Bhalla family trusts.

In a Nov. 26 release from its Gurugram Zonal Office detailing a Nov. 25 Provisional Attachment Order under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA), 2002, the agency quantified total alleged proceeds of crime at approximately ₹568 crore (~$66.8 million). Anil Bhalla and Gautam Bhalla were previously arrested under PMLA on Sept. 28, 2026, and remain in judicial custody; both remain presumed innocent pending trial.

Fourteen Years, Zero Plots, and 22 Staffless Shells

The money-laundering case stems from multiple First Information Reports filed by the Economic Offences Wing of Delhi Police under Indian Penal Code Sections 420, 406, and 120-B against Vatika Limited and promoter-directors Anil, Gautam, and Gaurav Bhalla.

According to investigators, seven purchaser entities paid roughly ₹266.95 crore upfront between 2010 and 2012—representing the full sale consideration—for residential plots in Vatika India Next (Sectors 84 and 85) and Vatika India Next-2 (Sector 88A) in Gurugram. After plot-wise agreements were signed in 2014 and 2015, the agency alleges, project layouts were revised, plots were renumbered and relocated, and land was repeatedly re-allotted to new buyers. In Vatika India Next-2, investigators said, not a single plot out of roughly 1.10 lakh square yards bought for ₹90 crore has been delivered after 14 years, leaving plots valued at ₹140.73 crore undelivered across the two townships.

Project land was allegedly parked across roughly 22 group companies that had no employees and no independent operations, serving primarily to hold land banks, pledge mortgages to lenders, and issue corporate guarantees while buyer cash flowed to unrelated group and promoter entities.

Sixty-Six Plots Sold Twice

The agency also flagged a 2024 transaction in which Scaler Ventures Business Solutions Pvt. Ltd. paid ₹473.18 crore under an agreement to sell and buy-back covering 165 plots totaling 53,648.94 square yards. Fourteen of those plots had allegedly already been booked by earlier customers before the contract was even signed.

“Vatika Limited in total sold 66 plots to other buyers and received Rs. 60 Cr. (approx.) despite having already sold them to and received consideration from M/s Scaler Ventures.” — Directorate of Enforcement, Gurugram Zonal Office (Nov. 26, 2026)

Investigators further allege that nine plots were allotted to Bayani Developers Pvt. Ltd., a Bhalla family company, against a token payment of ₹1.30 crore. Earlier Section 17 searches across seven premises had already frozen ₹22.58 crore in assets, including bank accounts, fixed-deposit receipts, a Mercedes-Benz GLC 300, and more than 1.3 kilograms of gold and diamond jewellery. The new attachment sweeps in properties held by Kanchan Bhalla, Anil Bhalla Family Trust-I, Anil Bhalla and Kanchan Bhalla Family Trust, and affiliated companies, including the Mehrauli farmhouse.
