BENGALURU — India’s Directorate of Enforcement has provisionally attached ₹702.70 crore (roughly $82.7 million) in flats and land linked to M/s Ozone Urbana Infra Developers Pvt. Ltd. and its chairman and managing director, Vasudevan S, escalating a federal money-laundering investigation into an alleged ₹1,305.11 crore homebuyer fund diversion across five residential projects.

Announced Nov. 26 by the agency’s Bengaluru Zonal Office under Provisional Attachment Order (PAO) No. 45 of 2026, dated Nov. 25 under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA), 2002, the new freeze builds on an earlier ₹423.37 crore attachment issued on Oct. 4, 2025 (PAO No. 29/2025). Combined provisional attachments in the probe now stand at ₹1,126.07 crore.

Subvention Promises and Incomplete Towers

The PMLA inquiry grew out of multiple First Information Reports registered under Indian Penal Code Sections 419, 420, and 120B by Karnataka Police and the Central Bureau of Investigation’s Economic Offences Wing-I in New Delhi, including filings initiated pursuant to directions of the Supreme Court of India.

According to the Enforcement Directorate, Ozone Group entities controlled and managed by Vasudevan S marketed apartments through subvention, buyback, and zero-down-payment schemes, assuring buyers that the developer would service pre-EMI and EMI bank installments until possession was handed over. The agency named five project entities alongside Vasudevan S: Ozone Urbana Infra Developers (Aqua-2 and Avenue), Ozone Infra Developers (Prime), Ozone Projects (Metrozone), Ozone Elegant Developers (WF-48), and Ozone Realtors (Polestar).

“Funds collected from homebuyers were not confined to the respective projects. Substantial amounts were diverted and routed through various Ozone Group entities by way of inter-corporate deposits and other financial transactions.” — Directorate of Enforcement, Bengaluru Zonal Office (Nov. 26, 2026)

Inter-Corporate Detours

Investigators allege that although homebuyers paid substantial sums and mortgage lenders disbursed housing loans directly to the builder entities, the apartments remained unfinished and undelivered while collections totaling roughly ₹1,305.11 crore (~$153.5 million) were siphoned across sister companies.

Rather than paying contractors and concrete suppliers, the agency alleges, the diverted funds went toward refinancing older loans, interest and finance charges, investor repayments, corporate overhead, and unrelated group liabilities. Because the action is a provisional attachment order and further investigation remains underway, the allegations have not been adjudicated at trial and the respondents retain the presumption of innocence.
