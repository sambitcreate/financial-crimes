DENVER — Federal securities regulators say an overseas-directed network turned Colorado’s corporate registry and federal disclosure portals into stage props for a rotating $37.4 million cryptocurrency investment scheme that ensnared more than 1,500 U.S. investors.

In a civil complaint filed Nov. 19 in the U.S. District Court for the District of Colorado (SEC v. Advisor WeChat Capital et al., Case No. 1:26-cv-06302), the Securities and Exchange Commission charged eight Colorado-registered entities organized into four back-to-back adviser-and-exchange pairs that operated between August 2024 and May 2026.

According to the agency, each pair followed the same seasonal playbook: run social-media ads funneling retail savers into WhatsApp and messaging groups, build rapport with ordinary U.S. stock commentary, then persuade victims to liquidate brokerage accounts, 401(k)s, and other assets to fund purported crypto offerings on sham trading websites.

Four Brand Names, One Playbook

To disarm skeptical investors, the complaint alleges, operators fabricated counterfeit S.E.C. registration certificates while directing targets to genuine administrative filings—such as EDGAR identifiers, Forms D, or Forms ADV—submitted to the S.E.C., FINRA, or FinCEN. After several months, each pairing shuttered and reappeared under fresh corporate names.

  • Aug.–Dec. 2024: Advisor WeChat Capital and Tech Brokerage Asset Alliance Ltd.
  • Jan.–July 2025: HB Wealth Advisors and IdeaCrypto Exchange LLC.
  • July–Dec. 2025: Convergent Wealth Advisors Inc. and RealSimple Crypto Exchange Inc.
  • Jan.–May 2026: Associated Wealth Advisors Ltd. and Essential Crypto Exchange.

The S.E.C. is seeking permanent injunctions, disgorgement of alleged ill-gotten gains with prejudgment interest, and civil penalties. The lawsuit is a contested civil enforcement action, and the agency’s allegations have not yet been adjudicated in court.
