SOFIA — Eight individuals, including five sitting municipal councillors in southern Bulgaria’s Haskovo municipality, were detained on Nov. 25 at the request of the European Public Prosecutor’s Office (EPPO) in Sofia over an alleged five-year scheme to siphon more than €70 million in European Union development and recovery grants.

In a press announcement issued from Luxembourg, EPPO said investigators raided the Haskovo municipal administration, private residences, and company offices alongside Bulgaria’s General Directorate Combating Organised Crime (GDBOP) and Anti-Corruption Commission (KPK). Searches yielded €135,000 and $9,000 in cash, gold coins, documents, mobile phones, and computers.

Municipal Officials on Both Sides of the Contract

According to prosecutors, the suspected scheme began in 2021 and continued until this week’s raids, targeting projects in which the municipality of Haskovo stood as beneficiary under the European Regional Development Fund (ERDF) and the EU’s post-pandemic Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRF).

The municipal projects under scrutiny included construction of a home for the elderly, a local arts centre, and installations of renewable energy systems. EPPO alleges that the five detained public officials initiated procurement procedures for those works and steered the resulting contracts to private companies in whose management or representation the same officials were allegedly involved—either jointly or individually—alongside three detained private company representatives.

Preliminary Losses Top €70 Million

Preliminary EPPO estimates place potential damage to the European Union budget at more than €70 million. The arrests mark the second major Bulgarian public-procurement action announced by EPPO in a single week, following a separate Sofia indictment over a €213.7 million railway modernisation grant. EPPO stressed that the Haskovo investigation remains ongoing and that all eight detained individuals are presumed innocent until proven otherwise in Bulgarian courts.
