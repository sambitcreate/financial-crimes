MUMBAI — Selling promised vacations turned out to be far more lucrative for the sales force than for the travelers. On Nov. 23, India’s Directorate of Enforcement (E.D.) announced the provisional attachment of immovable properties worth approximately ₹556.72 crore ($65.5 million) in its money-laundering investigation into Royal Twinkle Star Club Limited and Citrus Check Inns Limited.

The provisional attachment order, issued by the agency’s Mumbai Zonal Office-I under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (P.M.L.A.), stems from a police First Information Report registered at Mumbai’s R.A.K. Marg Police Station. According to the E.D.’s Nov. 23 press release, the companies mobilized public money through long-term holiday membership and investment plans that operated as unregistered Collective Investment Schemes without authorization from the Securities and Exchange Board of India (S.E.B.I.).

₹8,852 Crore Raised, ₹3,077 Crore Paid to Agents

The accounting trail laid out in the agency’s filing reads like a textbook diagram of an alleged Ponzi-type fund circulation machine. Between April 1, 2008, and March 31, 2017, Royal Twinkle Star Club and Citrus Check Inns collected approximately ₹8,852 crore (roughly $1.04 billion) from public investors. Of that sum, investigators traced approximately ₹3,430 crore in repayments to earlier investors—allegedly funded with fresh inflows from new enrollees—while a staggering ₹3,077 crore went out the door as commissions to intermediaries and agents who recruited members.

Another ₹1,723 crore was allegedly transferred to associated and sister concerns through loans, advances, and inter-corporate transactions. Investigators allege the cash was routed through a labyrinth of more than 87 group companies, firms, and entities controlled by promoter Om Prakash Goenka and members of his family, and deployed into real estate, hospitality, and related commercial ventures.

The E.D. stated that the ₹556.72 crore provisional attachment was executed in furtherance of ongoing proceedings before the Supreme Court of India with the goal of protecting investor recoveries. Under India’s P.M.L.A. framework, provisional attachments remain subject to confirmation by the Adjudicating Authority and appellate review, and the agency noted that further investigation remains in progress.
