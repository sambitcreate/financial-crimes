ATLANTA — A “non-depleting escrow account” is supposed to do one thing above all else: not deplete. In a federal civil complaint filed in Atlanta and announced on Nov. 25, the Securities and Exchange Commission alleges that Georgia resident Michael P. Hanlon and J. Streicher Financial, LLC took $15 million from a public-company advisory client under exactly that guarantee and promptly spent it elsewhere.

According to SEC Litigation Release No. 26695, filed Nov. 23 in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Georgia (Case No. 1:26-cv-07148-MHC), Hanlon and J. Streicher Financial induced the public company to transfer $15 million for securities investing under written agreements promising that the balance would never fall below the deposited principal. The firm allegedly promised a 2% monthly return—24% annualized—and assured the client it could retrieve the escrowed principal in as little as six months.

Real Estate, European Payouts, and an Auditor Reassured

Instead of holding the $15 million intact, the SEC alleges, Hanlon diverted the client’s funds to buy an ownership stake in a real-estate development joint venture for J. Streicher Financial’s parent company and to cover other unauthorized disbursements, including Ponzi-like payments to investors in the parent company’s failed venture in Europe. When questions arose, the complaint alleges, Hanlon misled both the public-company client and the company’s auditor into believing the escrowed $15 million was still untouched.

“The SEC encourages investors to watch for red flags of investment fraud, including unlicensed investment professionals and promises of guaranteed high returns.” — U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, Litigation Release No. 26695

The civil complaint charges Hanlon and J. Streicher Financial with violating Section 10(b) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 and Rule 10b-5, as well as Sections 206(1) and 206(2) of the Investment Advisers Act of 1940, and charges Hanlon in the alternative with aiding and abetting the firm’s advisory-act violations. Litigated by the SEC’s Atlanta Regional Office, the lawsuit seeks permanent injunctions and civil monetary penalties against both defendants plus disgorgement with prejudgment interest from Hanlon. The allegations remain contested civil claims in federal court.
