MELBOURNE — Nearly eight years after a retail derivatives broker that boasted of being Australia’s fourth-largest over-the-counter trading house collapsed with more than A$8.9 million owed to its customers, its former director’s bid to shorten his prison sentence hit a wall. On Nov. 23, the Full Federal Court refused Stavro D’Amore leave to appeal his jail term.

The decision leaves intact the three-year and ten-month total effective sentence—requiring 23 months in prison—handed down by Federal Court Justice Abraham on July 23, 2026, following an investigation by the Australian Securities and Investments Commission (A.S.I.C.) and prosecution by the Commonwealth Director of Public Prosecutions. D’Amore had pleaded guilty on May 1, 2026, to three rolled-up Corporations Act offences covering dishonest use of his position as a director, dishonest conduct in a financial services business, and authorizing false and misleading statements to the regulator.

Phantom Overseas Accounts and A$8.9 Million Unpaid

Between 2017 and 2018, D’Amore improperly transferred A$681,496.98 (approximately $443,000) in company funds—money he knew was derived primarily from retail client deposits—for purposes unrelated to Berndale Capital Securities Pty Ltd’s business. To satisfy Berndale’s Australian financial services license requirement to hold minimum net tangible assets and lodge audited reports, he authorized false statements to A.S.I.C. claiming company funds sat in overseas bank accounts that either did not exist or were grossly overstated.

Berndale imploded in December 2018, leaving retail clients out more than A$8.9 million. A second former director, Daniel Kirby, who was charged alongside D’Amore in June 2023, pleaded guilty in September 2024 and was sentenced in July 2025 to two years and eleven months in prison, to be released after 12 months on a A$1,000 good-behaviour recognisance.

“The Full Court’s decision leaves in place a significant prison sentence for serious and deliberate dishonesty,” A.S.I.C. Chair Sarah Court said in the agency’s Nov. 23 media release. “The dismissal of this application confirms the serious consequences that can follow when company directors deliberately abuse their position and attempt to conceal misconduct.”
