MUNICH — Germany’s federal banking regulator has once again discovered that a major Wall Street institution’s European hub struggled with the basics of anti-money laundering compliance—and responded with a stern homework assignment. On Nov. 20, BaFin announced an administrative order directing Munich-based State Street Bank International GmbH to remediate “numerous, in some cases serious and recurring shortcomings” in its money-laundering controls, accompanied by zero euro in announced fines.

In its public enforcement notice, the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority stated that State Street’s German banking subsidiary violated both the German Money Laundering Act (Geldwäschegesetz, or GwG) and the German Banking Act (Kreditwesengesetz, or KWG). Rather than a single isolated gap, BaFin catalogued breakdowns across six core compliance pillars: enterprise risk analysis, the duties of the anti-money laundering officer, transaction monitoring, the outsourcing of internal safeguards, the fulfillment of enhanced due diligence for higher-risk clients, and the regular updating of customer data.

Six Core Pillars, One Remediation Plan

Under German supervisory law, credit institutions must maintain an effective risk-management framework anchored in an accurate risk analysis, monitor outsourced compliance functions continuously, and refresh customer files more frequently whenever enhanced due diligence applies. BaFin’s order compels State Street Bank International to submit a formal remediation plan, correct each identified deficiency in accordance with that schedule, and file periodic progress reports with supervisors.

No monetary fine, business restriction, or individual executive sanction was announced alongside the Nov. 20 measure. For one of the largest institutional custodians operating in Europe, the immediate cost of “serious and recurring” statutory anti-money laundering violations remained strictly administrative: a binding promise to file a plan and keep the regulator updated.
