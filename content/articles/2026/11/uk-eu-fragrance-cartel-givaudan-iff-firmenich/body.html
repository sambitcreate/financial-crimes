LONDON — The world’s three largest fragrance makers were hit with coordinated antitrust fines totaling €471.97 million in Brussels and £85.17 million in London on Nov. 25 after admitting they spent four years swapping pricing strategies, resisting ingredient-cost transparency, and promising not to poach each other’s Unilever account staff—including over “beer and curry” dinners in London.

In twin decisions announced by the U.K. Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) and the European Commission (Case AT.40826), Swiss producers Givaudan SA and Firmenich International Sàrl alongside U.S.-based International Flavors & Fragrances Inc. (IFF) acknowledged participating in a continuous cartel from March 2019 until unannounced dawn raids disrupted the arrangement in March 2023.

WhatsApp Threads, ‘Beer and Curry’ Dinners, and a No-Poach Pact

Both regulators found that all three suppliers coordinated their commercial negotiations with Unilever—the single largest global purchaser of consumer fragrances for household staples such as laundry detergent, washing-up liquid, cleaning sprays, and shampoo—to rebuff price decreases and align on energy surcharges and transparency pushback. To keep the arrangement intact, the three rivals also agreed not to actively recruit employees working on each other’s Unilever accounts.

Separately, Givaudan and IFF went further, exchanging competitively sensitive pricing targets and negotiation updates across their broader consumer-fragrance and fine-perfume businesses, with senior employees of both firms directly participating from November 2021 onward. The CMA noted that the exchanges ran through WhatsApp messages, telephone calls, and in-person “beer and curry” meetings in London.

“These were major international suppliers discussing information that should have remained confidential. Today’s fines should be a clear warning for all businesses: competitors must compete, not coordinate behind closed doors.” — Juliette Enser, Executive Director of Competition Enforcement, U.K. CMA

Stacked Leniency Discounts and Zero Individual Charges

Because all three conglomerates lined up for leniency after the March 2023 raids and agreed to settle without appealing, regulators trimmed the final bills substantially. Under the European Commission’s settlement order, Givaudan received a 30% leniency cut and 10% settlement discount for a final fine of €252,363,000; Firmenich received 20% leniency, 10% settlement, and 5% mitigation for €137,689,000; and IFF received 50% leniency plus 10% settlement for €81,915,000.

In the parallel U.K. decision, the CMA granted a 65% leniency reduction to IFF and 40% to Givaudan and Firmenich, plus an additional 20% settlement discount each—leaving Givaudan with £39,960,000 across two infringements, IFF with £24,570,000 across two infringements, and Firmenich with £20,640,000. Both competition authorities noted they coordinated closely with the Swiss Competition Commission and the U.S. Department of Justice; neither decision imposed criminal liability or prison time on any individual executive.
