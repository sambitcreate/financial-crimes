ROME — European prosecutors and Italy’s financial police have swept across nine European Union Member States to execute a €14.5 million preventive seizure order against an alleged cross-border mobile-phone tax scheme that funded steep online retail discounts by simply keeping the Value Added Tax.

Announced Nov. 27 by the European Public Prosecutor’s Office (EPPO) in Rome, the coordinated operation by the Rome VI Nucleo Operativo Metropolitano of the Guardia di Finanza hit targets simultaneously in Belgium, Bulgaria, Estonia, France, Germany, Italy, Malta, Romania, and Slovakia. Officers froze more than €2.2 million in real estate, luxury items, cash, and bank balances while carrying out searches in Italy and Romania.

Discounted Phones, Abandoned Shells

The investigation grew out of a tax audit of an Italian telecommunications e-commerce company and examines suspected VAT fraud, money laundering, self-laundering, and fraudulent asset transfers involving four companies and 11 individuals. The preventive seizure order was issued by the Judge for Preliminary Investigations at the Court of Rome.

According to EPPO prosecutors, between 2019 and 2020 the group acquired large batches of mobile phones from suppliers elsewhere in the European Union and sold them online to Italian retail consumers at heavily discounted prices. The margin that made those discounts possible, investigators allege, came from collecting VAT at checkout and never remitting it to tax authorities.

“The VAT liabilities were left with inactive companies that had no real premises or genuine business activity. These companies were subsequently abandoned, leaving the tax debts unpaid. The resulting tax loss is estimated at €14.5 million.” — European Public Prosecutor’s Office (Nov. 27, 2026)

Luxury Cars and Round-Trip Transfers

Rather than leaving proceeds inside the paper companies holding the €14.5 million (~$15.7 million) tax bill, the suspects allegedly wired part of the money abroad before routing it back into Italy to acquire ostensibly legitimate businesses, while withdrawing other proceeds in cash or purchasing high-value real estate and luxury cars.

The EPPO noted that all 11 individuals and four companies covered by the preliminary seizure order are presumed innocent until proven otherwise before the competent Italian courts.
