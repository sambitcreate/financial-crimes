WÜRZBURG, Germany — In European value-added-tax law, declaring a brand-new smartphone “second-hand” is not a marketing misprint—it is a multi-million-euro tax arbitrage strategy. On Nov. 23, the European Public Prosecutor’s Office (E.P.P.O.) in Munich filed an indictment at the Regional Court of Würzburg against three individuals over an alleged cross-border electronics scheme estimated to have cost taxpayers €48 million ($52.2 million) in unpaid VAT.

Code-named Investigation Mela, the prosecution targets two individuals from Würzburg—registered as managing directors of a wholesale and retail electronics firm—alongside a co-managing director of another electronics trading company. According to the E.P.P.O.’s Nov. 23 announcement, the three defendants participated in a wider web of shell companies operating across several European Union Member States and the United Kingdom since 2018.

Abusing Margin Taxation on New Phones

Prosecutors allege the network systematically misused Europe’s special margin-taxation regime, which permits dealers in qualifying second-hand goods—items on which VAT has already been paid—to calculate tax solely on their resale profit margin rather than the full retail price. By falsely paper-trailing factory-new mobile phones and small electronic devices as used merchandise, the companies allegedly slashed their VAT liability and undercut legitimate competitors on price.

The indictment follows an October 2025 enforcement sweep across multiple jurisdictions that produced seven arrests and more than 100 property searches. During those raids, investigators seized business records, electronic devices, cash, mobile phones, jewelry, cars, watches, and gold valued at roughly €4 million—less than a tenth of the estimated €48 million tax hole.

As the E.P.P.O. emphasized in its filing notice, all defendants remain presumed innocent unless and until proven otherwise before the competent German courts.
