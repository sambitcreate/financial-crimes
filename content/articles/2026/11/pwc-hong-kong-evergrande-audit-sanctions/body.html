HONG KONG — After China Evergrande collapsed under mountains of debt, investors asked how years of clean audit stamps missed the missing floor. On April 23, 2026, Hong Kong regulators gave PricewaterhouseCoopers Hong Kong a HK$1.3 billion ($167 million) bill — only for Evergrande’s liquidators to slide an $8.4 billion invoice across the High Court bench.

The coordinated sanctions from Hong Kong’s Securities and Futures Commission and the Accounting and Financial Reporting Council combine a HK$300 million A.F.R.C. fine, a HK$1 billion S.F.C. compensation fund, and a six-month ban on accepting new Public Interest Entity audit clients. PwC did not admit liability in the S.F.C. agreement, and zero criminal charges were filed.

Two Invoices for the Same Stamps

In accounting, big regulatory checks are meant to buy closure: pay the fine, sit out a brief timeout, and go back to signing off on balance sheets. Against the liquidators’ US$8.4 billion High Court claim, however, the entire $167 million regulatory package comes to 1.99 cents on the dollar — a fifty-to-one gap.

As of November 2026, both the liquidators’ $8.4 billion civil suit and a legal challenge to the regulatory settlement itself remain contested in Hong Kong’s High Court, with no final judgment entered. Still, the math is awkward for PwC: settling with the referees rarely persuades the creditors holding an $8.4 billion tab to leave the table.
