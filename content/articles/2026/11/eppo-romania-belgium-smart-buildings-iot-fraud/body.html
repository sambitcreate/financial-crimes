IAȘI, ROMANIA — European prosecutors carried out 14 house searches across Romania and Belgium and seized nine properties worth roughly €1 million after alleging that a €1.1 million European Union “smart buildings” grant relied on a payroll of 19 phantom employees and a purchase of technical know-how that never happened.

Disclosed Nov. 27 by the European Public Prosecutor’s Office (EPPO) in Iași, the Nov. 26 raids were conducted jointly with Romania’s Economic Crime Investigation Service in Iași County, the County Gendarmerie Inspectorate, and Belgium’s Federal Police anti-corruption (OCRC/CDBC) and organized-crime (DJSOC) directorates. Three individuals were formally notified of their status as suspects and placed under judicial supervision.

An Internet of Nonexistent Things

The project under investigation was meant to develop and acquire equipment for managing smart buildings through an Internet of Things (IoT) platform. Its total budget stood at approximately €1.1 million (RON 6.2 million), backed by an EU funding commitment of roughly €987,850 (RON 5.2 million).

Investigators allege the beneficiary company drew more than €655,000 in EU disbursements by filing fictitious project expense claims — including wages for 19 individuals who had never actually worked on the project and a purported acquisition of technical know-how that never took place.

“It is understood that a substantial part of these funds was subsequently channelled, through various bank transfers and companies in Belgium and Luxembourg, back to the administrator of the beneficiary company for his personal benefit.” — European Public Prosecutor’s Office (Nov. 27, 2026)

Nine Properties Frozen in Iași County

To secure potential recovery of EU budget losses and judicial costs, prosecutors seized nine real-estate assets in Iași County belonging to the three suspects — five plots of land, two houses, and two apartments valued at approximately €1 million — and froze all bank accounts held by the beneficiary company.

The EPPO stated that the conduct under investigation could constitute subsidy fraud, forgery, and money laundering, emphasizing that all persons concerned are presumed innocent until proven otherwise in the competent Romanian courts.
