LONDON — A former company director whose missing books left liquidators unable to verify more than £1.1 million passing through her firm’s bank accounts has walked out of Southwark Crown Court with a six-month suspended prison sentence and 100 hours of unpaid work.

Sentenced on Nov. 26 following a prosecution by the U.K. Insolvency Service, Jenna Lennon, 41, had pleaded guilty in September to failing to deliver up the accounting records of Hope & Pride Limited (Company No. 11871782) under the Insolvency Act 1986. Her six-month custodial term was suspended for 12 months, and the agency announced no criminal fine.

An Unclassified Business and an Unverified Loan

Listed on Companies House under the admirably opaque classification of “other information service activities not elsewhere classified,” Hope & Pride Limited entered liquidation in September 2023 with Lennon as its sole director. Despite repeated requests from liquidators, Lennon produced no company books or accounting records whatsoever.

Without ledgers, invoices, or bank explanations, liquidators could not verify the nature or source of more than £1.1 million (~$1.39 million) paid into Hope & Pride’s accounts, nor establish the insolvent firm’s true liabilities. HM Revenue and Customs had separately calculated that £396,630 was owed on a Director’s Loan Account for funds paid out to Lennon—a balance liquidators were likewise unable to audit because the paperwork never materialized.

“Without adequate records, liquidators can be prevented from establishing what happened to company money, what is owed to creditors and whether funds were used for legitimate business purposes.” — The Insolvency Service (Nov. 26, 2026)

Seven-Year Ban, Zero Days Inside

Lennon had already received a seven-year director disqualification in March 2025 over the same record-keeping failure. Combined with Thursday’s Crown Court disposal, the ledger of consequences for £1.1 million in untraceable corporate cash flows totals a seven-year boardroom timeout, 100 hours of community service, and zero days of immediate imprisonment.
