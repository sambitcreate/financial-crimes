CHICAGO — The United States Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit affirmed the federal bribery conviction of former Illinois House Speaker Michael J. Madigan in April 2026, leaving intact a 92-month prison sentence and $2.5 million fine for an arrangement that effectively outsourced political patronage onto the payroll of the state’s largest electric utility.

Mr. Madigan, who presided over the Illinois House for decades, was originally sentenced in June 2025 in the United States District Court for the Northern District of Illinois to more than seven and a half years in federal prison alongside the $2.5 million monetary penalty. The criminal case centered on roughly $1.3 million in no-show jobs and allied payouts funded by Commonwealth Edison, known as ComEd, in connection with state utility legislation valued at more than $150 million to the company.

In a separate corporate resolution, ComEd previously paid $200 million to settle its own exposure arising from the bribery scheme — an outlay that exceeded the roughly $1.3 million in no-show patronage payments by more than 150 to one.

The Return on Ratepayer-Funded Patronage

Viewed strictly as a ledger of political capital, the numbers established in Chicago federal court reveal a striking disparity between the modest cost of patronage and the scale of legislative stakes in Springfield. Rather than funding political loyalists directly from campaign accounts, the scheme steered approximately $1.3 million in no-show ComEd arrangements toward allies connected to Mr. Madigan while legislation worth more than $150 million to the electric utility moved through the statehouse.

For ComEd, the arithmetic briefly looked extraordinarily favorable: $1.3 million in quiet subcontracted payroll stood opposite more than $150 million in legislative value. Once federal prosecutors in the Northern District of Illinois intervened, however, the ledger inverted sharply, saddle-stitching the utility with a $200 million corporate penalty and its most powerful legislative ally with a federal felony conviction.

In April 2026, the Seventh Circuit affirmed Michael J. Madigan’s Northern District of Illinois conviction and June 2025 sentence of 92 months in prison and a $2.5 million fine, upholding federal prosecutors’ case over $1.3 million in ComEd no-show jobs tied to $150 million-plus utility legislation.

Appellate Finality in Illinois Politics

The Seventh Circuit’s April 2026 ruling removed the principal legal obstacle to Mr. Madigan’s seven-year, eight-month prison term, closing an appellate chapter that political observers across Chicago and Springfield had watched as a referendum on how federal bribery law reaches entrenched legislative power.

Illinois has never lacked for cautionary tales at the intersection of public office and private money. Yet the Madigan-ComEd docket stands apart for its institutional neatness: a regulated monopoly underwriting $1.3 million in no-show positions, a legislative prize exceeding $150 million, a $200 million corporate reckoning, and a former House speaker whose 92-month sentence now carries the Seventh Circuit’s stamp of finality.
