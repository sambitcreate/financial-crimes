CHICAGO — The Seventh Circuit affirmed former Illinois House Speaker Michael J. Madigan’s bribery conviction in April 2026, locking in a 92-month federal prison term and $2.5 million fine for outsourcing his political patronage payroll to the state’s electric monopoly.

Mr. Madigan, who ran the Illinois House for decades, was sentenced in June 2025 in the Northern District of Illinois after prosecutors proved Commonwealth Edison funneled roughly $1.3 million in no-show subcontracts to his allies while legislation worth more than $150 million to the utility moved through Springfield.

Ratepayer Payroll, Inverted Math

For a while, the trade looked like a steal for ComEd: $1.3 million in quiet consulting retainers for ward captains opposite $150 million-plus in friendly utility bills — better than a hundred-to-one return on subcontracted patronage.

Then Chicago federal prosecutors arrived and flipped the ledger upside down. ComEd paid $200 million in a separate deferred prosecution deal — more than 150 times the no-show payroll itself — while Illinois’s longest-serving speaker drew seven and a half years in federal custody.

Illinois rarely runs short on statehouse corruption stories, but the Madigan-ComEd docket deserves a frame on the wall for sheer accounting clarity: $1.3 million in phantom jobs, $150 million in energy bills, a $200 million utility penalty, and an appellate stamp sending the speaker to prison.
