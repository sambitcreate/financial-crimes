MELBOURNE — The Australian Securities and Investments Commission has filed civil penalty proceedings in the Supreme Court of Victoria against AIG Australia Limited, alleging the insurer breached mandatory 30-day complaint-handling deadlines in 534 cases and relied on a standard delay-letter template that prepopulated “complexity” as the excuse.

Announced Nov. 26 in Media Release 26-294MR, the suit alleges contraventions of sections 912A(1)(g) and 912A(5A) of the Corporations Act 2001 spanning October 2021 through December 2025. According to the regulator, policyholders seeking answers on travel and consumer-device insurance disputes—including customers who were unwell, injured, or recently bereaved—were left waiting months for decisions, with one customer waiting up to 306 days.

Personal Drives and Pre-Filled Excuses

Under ASIC’s Regulatory Guide 271, introduced in October 2021, financial firms must generally provide written complaint responses or formal delay notices within 30 calendar days and tell dissatisfied customers how to escalate unresolved disputes to the Australian Financial Complaints Authority (AFCA).

ASIC alleges AIG missed that 30-day deadline 534 times, omitted AFCA escalation rights in 179 instances, and from at least April 2025 deployed a standard delay-communication template pre-filled with “complexity” as the stated reason for the holdup—allegedly misattributing delays to complaint complexity in nine cases. The regulator further alleges AIG’s internal tracking system neither generated alerts when responses fell due nor reliably recorded whether complaints had been answered at all, leading the insurer to materially understate missed deadlines in internal reporting.

“Insurers must have effective systems for recording complaints and should not have to search personal drives and emails to know whether a complaint was responded to.” — Alan Kirkland, ASIC Commissioner

After Warning the Industry

The suit follows ASIC’s December 2024 review of 11 general insurers (Report 802), which found firms failed to identify one in six complaints, and a May 2026 Federal Court finding against Telstra Super over separate dispute-handling delays.

In Victoria, ASIC is seeking declarations, pecuniary penalties, adverse publicity orders, and court-ordered engagement of an independent expert to overhaul AIG Australia’s complaints-handling controls. The civil allegations have not yet been tested at trial.
