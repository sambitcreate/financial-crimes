NEW YORK — Federal prosecutors in Manhattan unsealed an indictment in November 2026 charging two men behind an investment firm named Backward Hat Capital with operating an approximately $600 million foreign-exchange Ponzi scheme that drew in more than 2,000 retail investors.

According to the criminal indictment filed by the United States Attorney’s Office for the Southern District of New York, defendants Phillip Williams and Devin Johnson solicited hundreds of millions of dollars from individual savers by promising investment returns exceeding 6 percent per month — equivalent to more than 100 percent per year — through purported currency trading. The case remains pending trial, and both men are presumed innocent unless and until proven guilty in court.

Even in a financial district accustomed to aggressive marketing pitches, prosecutors’ filings describe an enterprise whose outward branding did little to disguise its informality. Rather than adopting the restrained nomenclature of traditional asset managers, the firm operated openly under the banner of Backward Hat Capital while collecting roughly $600 million from retail participants.

Compounding Beyond the Global Economy

The mathematical implications of the promised returns sit at the center of the government’s allegations. Prosecutors allege that Mr. Williams and Mr. Johnson lured more than 2,000 investors with assurances of steady monthly gains above 6 percent in foreign-exchange markets, an arena notorious among institutional trading desks for razor-thin margins and abrupt currency swings.

A straightforward compounding calculation illustrates the scale of what investors were allegedly told was routine. An initial investment compounding reliably at 6 percent every month — more than doubling each year — would not merely outperform every sovereign wealth fund and quantitative hedge fund in history; within 18 years, that trajectory would mathematically eclipse the gross domestic product of the entire planet.

According to the Southern District of New York indictment, the defendants solicited roughly $600 million from more than 2,000 retail investors on promises of monthly foreign-exchange returns exceeding 6 percent — a rate of compounding unsustainable in legitimate currency markets.

A Classic Structure in Modern Dress

According to the indictment, the underlying mechanics of Backward Hat Capital bore little resemblance to sophisticated foreign-exchange execution. Prosecutors allege that the enterprise functioned as a classic Ponzi structure, relying on fresh inflows from newly recruited retail investors to sustain the illusion of extraordinary trading profits rather than generating legitimate currency returns.

The Southern District of New York’s November 2026 unsealing places the case among the largest retail investment fraud indictments brought in Manhattan in recent years by total funds allegedly raised. Because the proceedings are at the criminal indictment stage and awaiting trial, the government’s claims have not yet been tested before a jury. Still, the docket serves as a dry reminder of an enduring market constant: when an investment pitch promises annual returns above 100 percent, the most revealing due diligence often requires nothing more advanced than a pocket calculator.

By the Numbers

  • ~$600 million: Total funds prosecutors allege were raised by Backward Hat Capital.

  • Pending trial: Criminal proceedings in the Southern District of New York following the November 2026 unsealing; all charges are allegations.
